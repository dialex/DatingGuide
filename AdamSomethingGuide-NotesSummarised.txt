# Dating

---

## Step: Call to book the date

**Description**
Wait 4–5 days after getting their number, then call. Hearing their voice tells you how interested they are.

**Tips**
- Call on a workday, ideally 18:00–20:00, when they're home from work and relaxed.
- Keep small talk to a couple of minutes. The phone is for setting dates, not chatting.
- Be assertive. "I want to take you out on a date. How about [day] at [time] at [place]?"
- If they don't pick up, don't call again right away. It reads as desperate.
- Send one text instead. "Hi [name], this is [your first name]. Call me back on this number: [number]. Have a nice day."
- Wait 3–4 days for them to call back. If nothing, try once more. If still nothing, move on.

---

## Step: Set when and where

**Description**
Set the date 4–5 days, maximum one week, in advance. Too soon looks presumptuous, too late looks half-hearted.

**Tips**
- Pick a workday from Monday to Thursday. You're still strangers, so weekends and Fridays are off-limits.
- Meet after work hours, ideally 18:00–20:00. An earlier time may clash with their schedule.
- Choose a safe, accessible venue where they'll feel comfortable.
- Say on the phone whether the date involves a meal. This avoids them arriving already fed or starving.

---

## Step: Book a coffee date

**Description**
A coffee date is a short, low-cost first meeting. A safe, neutral spot to see if there's a spark.

**Tips**
- Meet at a café in a busy, safe area.
- Go after work, in the afternoon or evening.
- Keep it to about 30 minutes.
- Stick to 1–2 non-alcoholic drinks each. You pay as the host.
- Everyone gets there and leaves on their own.
- One coffee date per person, ever. Anything more reads as cheap.

---

## Step: Book a regular date

**Description**
If you already talked 20–30 minutes when you met and you're sure there's a spark, you can open with a regular date instead. It's a longer, costlier affair than coffee.

**Tips**
- Meet at a restaurant in a busy, safe area.
- Go in the evening and plan for 2–3 hours.
- It's a full meal and you pay as the host.
- Always reserve a table in advance.
- Meet near the restaurant or offer to pick them up.
- For the first 4–5 dates avoid clubs and cinemas. You're there to talk and get to know each other.

---

## Step: Handle a broken date

**Description**
If they break a date without offering to reschedule, it's over. Move on.

**Tips**
- A no-show with no message means they aren't interested. Don't seethe over it. It's their loss.
- Cancelling shortly before with no alternative offered is also a no.
- If they come back later, apologise sincerely, and suggest a new time and place, that's acceptable.

---

## Step: Arrive at the date

**Description**
Show up 10–15 minutes early. Don't be late.

**Tips**
- If something unforeseen delays you, text them to let them know.
- When you meet, smile, say hi, and head to the venue right away.
- If they're late, wait 15 minutes, then text that you're on the spot waiting.
- If they don't show within another 15 minutes, give up and move on.

---

## Step: Behave at the table

**Description**
Order something simple and keep your manners sharp.

**Tips**
- Let your date order for themselves.
- Don't order alcohol for yourself.
- Don't interrupt them when they're talking.
- Don't talk with your mouth full.
- Don't brag about your accomplishments.
- Don't be rude to the staff.

---

## Step: Keep the conversation going

**Description**
Ask open-ended questions and let them do most of the talking. Listen and remember what they say.

**Tips**
- If you're extroverted, don't overwhelm them. If you're introverted, let them lead.
- Circle back to something they said earlier to score points. ("You said you played volleyball. Does your friend Brandon still play?")
- Avoid self-deprecating humour.
- Don't talk about your future together.
- Don't overshare about yourself.
- Don't brag.
- Steer clear of negative subjects.

---

## Step: End the date

**Description**
Aim for 1.5–2 hours and end on a high note, while both of you still want more.

**Tips**
- Wait for a lull, ideally when a topic is exhausted, then say "I had a nice time."
- Gesture for the waiter and pay the bill. As the host, you cover it.
- If they insist on pitching in, politely decline.
- Leave the venue together and offer to escort them back to their departure point.
- Say goodbye with a kiss. On the lips means they're into you, on the cheek means a friend for now, a quick hug or nothing means move on.
- Wish them goodnight and leave.

---

## Step: Stay off the phone between dates

**Description**
Don't contact them between dates except to set up the next one, which you'll do in 4–5 days.

**Tips**
- If they reach out first, forget the 4–5 days and invite them out right then. Their initiative is the signal.
- Use the phone for setting dates only, never chit chat.
- Save conversation topics for the date itself.
- Romantic feelings grow face-to-face, not over the phone.

---

## Step: Walk away when it's not right

**Description**
Tell minor annoyances apart from dealbreakers. Annoyances stack up, dealbreakers end things before they begin.

**Tips**
- A minor annoyance is fine if it's not a dealbreaker for you, but remember they add up and can breed resentment.
- A dealbreaker is an issue you can't get over. Don't try to ignore or rationalise it.
- If a dealbreaker surfaces on a date, stay calm and avoid drama. Finish the date, say goodbye politely, and move on.
- If they call or text afterwards, tell them a former long-term partner came back into your life.
