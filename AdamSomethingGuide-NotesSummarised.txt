# Keeping

---

## Step: Become exclusive

**Description**
Exclusivity usually just happens. You grow closer until you only want to see each other and nobody else.

**Tips**
- They may signal readiness by talking about you meeting their parents or friends, or by asking where the two of you stand.
- Treat those "couple things" as your cue to seal the deal.
- If you only want something casual, say so in the first few dates so you don't waste their time.

---

## Step: Drop everyone else

**Description**
The moment you become exclusive, stop seeing and contacting anyone else you were dating. No exceptions.

**Tips**
- If you'd only had a date or two, just stop contacting them.
- If you'd dated more, tell them you enjoyed it but someone asked you to be exclusive and you accepted. Then don't contact them again.
- Think how you'd feel if your partner still chatted casually with people they used to date. Keep it clean and clear-cut.

---

## Step: Keep the relationship alive

**Description**
Relationships need constant maintenance. Keep doing the things that made you fall for each other.

**Tips**
- Don't treat getting together as "mission accomplished" and stop making an effort.
- Keep dating. Have casual, romantic fun together at least once every 2–3 weeks.
- It doesn't have to be expensive. Quality time that lets you bond is what counts.
- Stay relaxed around each other but don't go lazy on grooming or habits.
- Raise what bothers you gently and tactfully so your partner can do the same.

---

## Step: Meet the parents

**Description**
Meeting the parents means being seen as someone who might join the family one day. Behave accordingly.

**Tips**
- Dress up nicely.
- Bring a small gift and check with your partner about it beforehand.
- Keep the mood positive.
- Behave like someone you'd want your own children to bring home.
- Never get drawn into political debate. It's the fastest way to get kicked out of the house.

---

## Step: Live together

**Description**
Living together exposes you to each other's worst traits every day. It takes constant effort to avoid becoming roommates.

**Tips**
- Know what annoys your partner and avoid those triggers.
- Don't stop dating. Get quality time out of the routine every 1–2 weeks.
- Keep a private space of your own if you're home a lot.
- Stay clean and tidy, smell nice, and clean up after yourself.
- Agree upfront on who does which chores and when, and keep it balanced.

---

## Step: Never escalate an argument

**Description**
Sooner or later you'll argue. Even winning an argument leaves the other person resentful.

**Tips**
- Stay calm and polite. Never be the one to start yelling.
- Show you listened and understood before restating your position.
- If you still disagree, hold your ground politely. Don't be dismissive or smug.
- Remember you love this person and chose to live with them.
- To end a deadlock, say "We'll just agree to disagree about that."

---

## Step: Manage money together

**Description**
Money is central to a relationship and often the main source of conflict. Agree how you'll manage it.

**Tips**
- Open a shared account where both send a set amount each month, used for anything that benefits you both.
- You each keep your own money to spend as you like.
- Both of you stay informed about your finances.
- Agree on a spending threshold above which you check with each other first.
- Track income and expenses so there are no nasty surprises.
- If money problems hit, make a plan together and stick to it.
- If your partner is careless with money, teach them gently instead of judging.

---

## Step: Watch for warning signs

**Description**
A healthy relationship needs balance. Watch for signs your partner is being turned off before it's too late.

**Tips**
- They touch you less and feel more distant, physically and mentally.
- They kiss you and show affection less often.
- They get irritated and argue with you more easily.
- They're less enthusiastic about sex.
- Act on these signs early. Once someone is fully disillusioned, nothing can fix it.

---

## Step: Go back to basics

**Description**
If you spot trouble, behave like you did during the dating phase.

**Tips**
- Make yourself a little more scarce by spending more time away from home.
- Take your partner on a date every week and keep it light and positive.
- Avoid arguments and be on your best behaviour.
- If it's you being turned off, calmly raise the specific things that bother you.
- If those things won't change, find ways to limit your exposure to them.
- Aim to be like you both were on the first few dates.

---

## Step: Handle being dumped

**Description**
Getting dumped feels horrible. When it's over, it's over. Once they're ready to let you go, it's done for good.

**Tips**
- Don't fight them on it, argue, try to change their mind, or cause a scene.
- Calmly acknowledge it and accept it. You have no choice.
- Discuss what happens to shared property.
- Discuss who moves out if you live together.

---

## Step: Break it off yourself

**Description**
How you end things depends on how far the relationship has gone.

**Tips**
- After only 1–3 dates you're still strangers. Just stop calling and inviting them out.
- If they reach out, say an old ex came back and you took them back. It's a kind white lie that spares everyone drama.
- After more than 3 dates but not exclusive, you owe them a phone call with that same apology.
- If you're exclusive, you owe them an in-person sit-down and apology.
- Don't show up with a list of their faults. Give a reason they can't argue with, like "my feelings are just gone somehow."

---

## Step: After the breakup talk

**Description**
Once you've said it, stick around for about half an hour.

**Tips**
- If they get upset, curse, or rage at you, let them. Then you're gone for good.
- If they get violent, leave immediately and never look back.
- Sort out items, finances, and the apartment later, once you've both calmed down.
- That part can be handled by text or phone call.

---

## Step: Sever all ties and move on

**Description**
Exit the relationship as cleanly as possible. One of you moves out, items are exchanged, and accounts and subscriptions are sorted. Cut every tie.

**Tips**
- The easiest way to get over someone is to replace them with someone new.
- Meet friends and family for support and talk it through.
- Examine what went wrong and keep yourself composed and stable.
- Get back into dating as soon as you can. Go to social outings, install an app, ask for numbers, and have fun.
